Software

Code and tools

Like many fields, systems biology has no single required tool: there are many ways to build a model and fit it to data. But standardizing the workflows behind that work, for the sake of repeatability and reliability, takes tools, and good tools make seemingly intangible tasks, such as building complex models and parameterizing them, more tangible.

These tools are for anyone interested in the mechanisms behind why things happen (e.g., how diseases spread, or why diseases like cancer arise) who wants to use computational modeling to fill gaps in our knowledge, or to predict how these systems will behave.

Lead developer

FluBNF

Python · MIT licence · 2026–present

In use weekly

An interface for using PyBNF to fit epidemiological models, for both retrospective analyses and real-time forecasts.

My part: Main developer. Developed FluBNF (an open-source, PyBNF-based influenza forecasting tool) and now submit weekly forecasts of laboratory-confirmed influenza hospital admissions using two models to the CDC FluSight challenge for the 2026-27 season.

LosAlamos_NAU-CModel_Flu, 2025–26

BNGL and PyBNF · Los Alamos National Laboratory and NAU

2025–26 season

A compartmental SIR model with a time-dependent transmission rate, fitted to hospitalization data by Bayesian inference in PyBNF, submitted to CDC FluSight for the 2025–26 season.

My part: As a graduate student and teaching assistant, I made the team's submissions with an earlier model and pipeline. After I received the NIH T32 G-RISE traineeship, that time went into building FluBNF, and a new model and algorithm, for the 2026–27 FluSight season.

Contributor

PyBioNetFit (PyBNF)

Python · Los Alamos National Laboratory

Manuscript in prep

Parameterizes biological models using quantitative data (e.g., time series), qualitative data (e.g., inequality constraints), and a likelihood function.

My part: Co-developer. Implemented a Liu–West particle filter for sequential Bayesian parameter estimation in PyBNF (now public in the Los Alamos National Laboratory PyBioNetFit (LANL/PyBNF) repository as of October 2026).

BNGsim

C++17 and Python · Los Alamos National Laboratory

Manuscript in prep

An embeddable simulation library for biochemical reaction networks.

My part: Co-developer. Designed and implemented a multi-engine benchmark suite for BNGsim (an open-sourced C++17 simulation engine for BioNetGen rule-based models) to compare its performance with other simulators, including libRoadRunner, AMICI, COPASI, and SciPy; ran the benchmarks; and helped with debugging.

  • lanl/bngsim

MicroHub

R and Shiny · Dr. Spencer Fox's group, NAU

A forecasting application written in R and Shiny, inspired by collaborative forecasting hubs like FluSight.

My part: Tester and trainer. Used MicroHub for international forecast submissions and co-delivered technical trainings on it with the CDC and PAHO in 2026. Also enhanced the MicroHub application through improvements to the user interface, increased functionality, and expanded capabilities, including automatically generated reports, producing time-series plots, supporting multiple languages, etc.
