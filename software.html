Software

Code and tools

Like many fields, systems biology has no single required tool: there are many ways to build a model and fit it to data. But standardizing the workflows behind that work, for the sake of repeatability and reliability, takes tools, and good tools make seemingly intangible tasks, such as building complex models and parameterizing them, more tangible.

These tools are for anyone interested in the mechanisms behind why things happen (e.g., how diseases spread, or why diseases like cancer arise) who wants to use computational modeling to fill gaps in our knowledge, or to predict how these systems will behave.
